About me

I'm a systems engineer with more than fifteen years of technical experience and an operations background most engineers don't have. I've run my own business, delivered IT for clients across five industries, and today help automate and operate a 1,400-host enterprise environment at the University of Dayton. I bring the technical depth to build platforms well and the business sense to make sure they're worth building.

The slightly longer version

I got my start in 2009 behind the counter at DNA Computers, fixing hardware and software problems for customers during the Great Recession and learning early how to do a lot with very little. While earning a business degree in operations management at Ohio State, I spent a corporate internship at Speedway, where I ran a regional compliance program and presented recommendations to the company's executive leadership. After graduating, I went back to DNA Computers to run operations and launch product lines that grew revenue.

That entrepreneurial streak led me to start my own IT services business, A Plus PC Doctor, and it's never really left me: I'm happiest when I'm building something from nothing and watching it work. Linux and open source software feed that instinct better than anything else, because they put every layer in my hands. From my own business I moved to a managed service provider, delivering IT for clients in higher education, finance, healthcare, non-profit, and manufacturing.

That led me to the University of Dayton, first as an IT Systems Analyst, where I built the department's asset management platform and later migrated it to Linux and Docker with zero downtime, and since 2025 as a Systems Engineer, where I work on the automation behind hundreds of Linux servers. Whether it's a business, a platform, or the site you're reading now, the reward for me is the same: taking an idea, building it well, and seeing it hold up in the real world.
